## en_x_01 — Tip

200 FPS and a 60 Hz monitor describe different parts of the pipeline.

Before buying another GPU, compare:
1. sustained FPS (not the peak)
2. monitor refresh rate
3. the FPS you actually want

The visible bottleneck may be the display—or there may be no urgent bottleneck.

## en_x_03 — Short thread (1/3–3/3)

**1/3** A 240 Hz monitor cannot create missing frames. A faster GPU cannot make a 60 Hz panel show 240 distinct refreshes. So “GPU or monitor?” begins with two measurements: sustained FPS and monitor Hz.

**2/3** If FPS is already above the display ceiling, investigate the monitor. If FPS is below the goal, lower settings in the same scene and diagnose CPU/GPU limits first.

**3/3** If both already match the experience you want, spend $0. The most efficient upgrade is the one that removes a real constraint.

## en_x_04 — Myth

Myth: “32GB RAM will increase FPS.”

Better version: 32GB can help if 16GB is genuinely under pressure from the game + browser + streaming/editing workload.

If memory is not the limit, more capacity may not be the highest-value gaming purchase.

## en_x_05 — Stop the replacement

Before replacing a gaming PC, test one repeatable scene at lower graphics settings.

Big FPS improvement → graphics-side limit is plausible.
Little change → check CPU, frame caps, temperatures, background work, and engine limits.

One test can prevent a very expensive guess.

## en_x_06 — Storage distinction

SSD upgrade outcomes should be described precisely:

✅ faster loading can be realistic
✅ better asset delivery can be realistic
❌ higher average FPS is not guaranteed

Fix the symptom you actually have, not the component with the easiest marketing number.

## en_x_07 — Gaming laptop

Gaming laptop upgrade checklist:

- Is RAM replaceable or soldered?
- Is there a free storage slot?
- Is performance limited by heat or power?
- Can the CPU/GPU be changed at all?

If the core limit is not upgradeable, do not stack small purchases and expect a new GPU experience.
